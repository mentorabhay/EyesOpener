Eyes Opener Showcase

Eyes Opener · showcase · dark pages

Two eyes. One picture.

Eyes Opener is a fitness app for your eyes. It tracks how you use them, checks them at home and trains them with games, for children and grown-ups, on a computer, a phone or a TV. This book walks through it screen by screen.

Eye care that feels like a fitness app

The home page says what Eyes Opener is in a few lines: check-ups, games, exercises and a community, for children and grown-ups, at home.

  • For families, counsellors and schools
  • Every game and exercise rests on published research
  • Not a medical device: it says when to see an eye doctor

02

How it works, in four steps

Track, Test, Train and Together. Each step opens to say what happens and how long it takes.

  • Set up once: the screen's size and the glasses' colours
  • A short check-up gives a starting point
  • Practice is measured, so the numbers show what changed

Computer · /how-it-works

03

The whole library, before you sign in

Anyone can browse every game, exercise and check-up, with what each one is for and how strong the evidence is.

  • Search and filter by condition
  • Each item names its evidence level
  • Nothing is hidden behind a sign-up

Computer · /library

04

Evidence first

Every method is graded from strong to unproven, with the trials behind it. Items with weak evidence say so.

  • Strong: two-eye video, outdoor light, patching support
  • Moderate and limited methods are marked as such
  • Sources are linked on every item

Computer · /evidence

05

No passwords

Sign in with Google, or with a link sent to your email. There is no password to remember, and none to leak.

  • A family stays signed in for 30 days
  • Staff and counsellors sign in again every 12 hours
  • Sign out everywhere ends every device at once

Computer · /login

22

What to do next

From the check-up results and published limits, the app says whether to see an eye doctor. It never says what a condition is.

  • Limits from paediatric screening guides and WHO
  • A clear next step, not a diagnosis
  • Clinic readings can be added by a parent or counsellor

Together, without a feed

Cheer someone who is practising with a light, a letter only both eyes can read, or a shared streak. No followers, no likes, no scrolling.

  • Grandparents can cheer from their own seat

29

Ideas that are tested before they are trusted

Members share what helped them as an idea others can try. Each climbs a ladder of six steps, from a story to a checked result.

  • Untested ideas are marked untested
  • A pooled result needs 30 members in each group
  • A hazard list blocks unsafe ideas

30

A league that ranks showing up

Teams are ranked by the days they finished their practice. Nothing else counts, so no one is pushed past a safe dose.

  • Small teams by age band
  • A new week every Monday
  • Never a score or a speed
